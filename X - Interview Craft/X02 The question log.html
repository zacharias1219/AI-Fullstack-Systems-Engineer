The question log

How do you turn every missed interview or drill question into a permanent, engineer-voiced answer instead of repeating the same blank-out two weeks later? This page covers the 7-column capture schema, engineer-voice model answers, and the 7-day to 30-day retest loop.

Study path

Before you read

Guess first. A wrong guess now makes the right answer stick later.

You miss "How do you optimize tokens?" in a drill, write down a strong model answer that evening, and never retest it. In the kata's 28-day cold-recall simulation, roughly what percentage of that answer survives compared to running a single 7-day cold retest?
Only 17.4% survives without a retest, compared to 74.7% with a 7-day cold retest (and 9.7% if you only re-read notes). Capturing a miss fixes the encoding for today, not retrieval a month from now.
Why does waiting 48 hours after an interview to log missed questions cut the log's usefulness almost in half, even if you still remember the general topic?
Because after 24 hours you remember the topic label ("token optimization") but forget the exact wording of the prompt and the exact boundary where your explanation stalled. Without the exact prompt and missing mechanism, you end up studying what you already know.
If you log 6 new misses per week and pass 70% of your 7-day retests on the first try, how many total retest items per week do you have to run in steady state?
About 14.6 items per week: 8.6 seven-day attempts (6 divided by 0.70) plus 6 thirty-day checks. At 2.5 minutes per cold answer, that is roughly 36 minutes per week to hold an entire curriculum cold.

The core idea

Most candidates treat a bad interview question like a bruise: they feel annoyed, read a blog post about the topic, nod along, and forget the exact mechanism ten days later. Reading creates familiarity, not cold recall. When the same question returns in a real loop with a slightly different framing, you stall at the exact same spot.

The question log turns every stumble into a tracked bug with a regression test. You capture the exact prompt and the exact missing piece on the same day, write a tight engineer-voiced model answer (mechanism, number, failure mode), and force yourself to speak it cold at day 7 and day 30 before marking the gap closed.

Memory hook

C-M-R-G (Catch, Model, Retest, Graduate): Catch the exact prompt and missing piece in under 24h (columns 1 to 5), write the Model answer with Mechanism + Number + Failure mode (column 6), Retest cold at 7 days (column 7), and Graduate to Solid only after passing day 30.

How it works

1. The 7-column schema and why every column pulls weight

Your tracker's Question log tab has seven tab-separated columns: Date, Question, Topic, Level reached, What was missing, Model answer, and Retest due. Each column prevents a specific study failure. Question stores the exact cold prompt (not a chapter title) so you can retest yourself without rereading notes. Topic links the miss to one master-index ID like G01 or K06 so weak domains surface automatically. Level reached (0, 1, or 2) forces honest grading against the three-level rubric from X01, and What was missing pins down the single gap you actually dropped.

2. The 24-hour capture rule and cue fidelity

After an interview or mock, memory of what you failed to say decays faster than memory of the general subject. Within 24 hours, you can still name the exact follow-up that broke your flow, such as forgetting why causal attention limits prompt caching to a prefix. Two days later, your brain smooths the stumble into "review token optimization." That vague entry breaks retrieval practice because it gives you no cold prompt to answer and no falsifiable gap to check. Same-day capture takes two minutes per row and preserves the exact failure boundary.

Check: Why is logging "Studied KV cache (K06)" worse than logging "Why does KV cache memory grow with batch size?" even if you spend 30 minutes reading notes on K06?
A topic label triggers passive recognition ("I recognize those words") instead of active recall. Without a specific question and a named gap, you cannot test whether you can actually produce the mechanism and formula aloud under pressure.

3. Auditing model answers for engineer voice

A model answer in column 6 is not an essay; it is a 2 to 4 sentence spoken target you can check in 10 seconds. Every row must pass the three-part engineer-voice audit from V01: one concrete mechanism (how it works), one number (a ratio, latency, memory size, or limit), and one failure mode (where it breaks). If a draft says "PagedAttention improves GPU efficiency by 60% and lowers spend," it scores 0.33 (number only, zero mechanism, zero failure mode). Rewriting that row before you close the session stops you from memorizing polished business fluff.

4. The 7-day and 30-day spacing mechanics

Forgetting follows an exponential decay curve: R(t) = exp(-t / S), where t is days elapsed and S is memory stability in days. Passive re-reading leaves S small (about 12 days in our model, leaving 9.7% cold recall at day 28). Writing a model answer once raises initial stability slightly (about 18 days, or 17.4% at day 28). Running one effortful cold retrieval at day 7 triggers the testing effect and multiplies stability (to roughly 70 to 95 days when the answer is well-structured), holding 74.7% cold recall at day 28.

R(t) = exp(-t / S)
Steady-state weekly retest items = M * (1 / p + 1)
Weekly retest minutes = M * (1 / p + 1) * T
where M = new misses/week, p = 7-day pass rate, T = minutes/item

Why does the workload stay bounded instead of growing forever? Because every miss passes through two finite gates: an average of 1 / p attempts to clear the 7-day gate, plus 1 check at day 30. Once it clears day 30 at Level 2 or higher, it leaves the active queue and moves to Solid. Use the explorable below to test where the weekly queue tips over a 30-minute budget.

5. Turning the log into a weak-domain heatmap

Once your log holds 15 or more rows, stop scanning it top to bottom. Group rows by the first letter of the Topic ID (for example G:2, K:2, X:1) and sort due items with overdue rows first. That turns a flat spreadsheet into a priority scheduler: if domain K (inference hardware) or G (prompting and tokens) holds 40% of your Level 1 misses, your next mock round targets those two domains instead of re-drilling topics you already pass.

Check: In the steady-state formula M * (1/p + 1), what happens to your weekly queue when you write weak model answers that drop your 7-day pass rate p from 0.80 to 0.40?
The multiplier (1/p + 1) jumps from 2.25 to 3.50, a 56% jump in weekly workload for the exact same number of new misses. Bad model answers create a retry storm in your own study queue.

import math
from datetime import date, timedelta
from collections import Counter

# --- Break-it flags (flip one at a time, predict, then run) ---
DELAYED_CAPTURE_HOURS = 0   # >24h blurs the exact gap you missed
GENERIC_NOTES_MODE = False  # True logs "studied X" with no cold prompt or model answer
SKIP_7D_GATE = False        # True jumps straight to day 30 with no day-7 retrieval

TODAY = date(2026, 11, 27)  # 7 days after logging Week 1 misses on 2026-11-20
MECH_KWS = ("prefix", "kv", "cache", "causal", "attention", "linear", "batch", "level", "budget", "hash")
NUM_KWS = ("%", "ms", "gb", "2x", "4x", "7d", "30d", "90", "20", "45", "25", "8", "5s", "50", "4096")
FAIL_KWS = ("miss", "breaks", "fails", "oom", "stale", "drop", "stall", "leak", "cap", "cost", "latency")

# 7-column Question Log: Date, Question, Topic, Level, What was missing, Model answer, Retest due
RAW_LOG = [
    ("2026-11-20", "How do you optimize tokens?", "G01", 1,
     "Prefix caching causal constraint and output-token 4x cost ratio",
     "Keep static prefix first so KV cache hits cut input cost 50%+; cap output tokens since decode is 4x input price; breaks if timestamps sit in system prompt.", "2026-11-27"),
    ("2026-11-20", "Why does prompt caching only work on a prefix?", "G02", 1,
     "Causal attention mask dependency",
     "Causal attention computes each token KV state from all prior tokens; one changed byte at index 10 invalidates 100% of the suffix cache and causes a full prefill miss.", "2026-11-27"),
    ("2026-11-20", "Why does KV cache memory grow with batch size?", "K06", 1,
     "Linear scaling with batch*seq and OOM threshold",
     "Each token stores K and V tensors across layers, scaling linearly with batch*seq; at 4096 ctx and batch 32, fp16 cache hits GBs and causes OOM.", "2026-11-27"),
    ("2026-11-20", "How does PagedAttention cut memory waste?", "K07", 1,
     "No mechanism or failure mode (business-voiced draft)",
     "It improves GPU efficiency by 60% and lowers serving spend for enterprise customers.", "2026-11-27"),
    ("2026-11-20", "How do you structure a 90-second system answer?", "X01", 2,
     "Missed failure mode in Level 3 internals",
     "Split into Level 1 outcome (20s), Level 2 mechanism (45s), Level 3 internals and limits (25s); fails when business ROI replaces mechanism.", "2026-11-27"),
    ("2026-11-13", "How do you prevent an agent tool loop?", "F02", 1,
     "Step budget cap and idempotency hash",
     "Enforce a max step budget (8 steps), tool timeout (5s), and state hash check; without a cap, repeated errors stall latency and leak token spend.", "2026-11-20"),
]

def audit_row(row):
    q, ans = row[1], row[5]
    if GENERIC_NOTES_MODE:
        q, ans = f"Studied {row[2]}", f"Reviewed notes on {row[2]} and understood it"
    low = ans.lower()
    has_mech = any(k in low for k in MECH_KWS)
    has_num = any(k in low for k in NUM_KWS)
    has_fail = any(k in low for k in FAIL_KWS)
    fidelity = 0.55 if DELAYED_CAPTURE_HOURS > 24 else 1.0
    score = (has_mech + has_num + has_fail) / 3.0 * fidelity
    return score, (has_mech, has_num, has_fail)

def simulate_4w_retention(avg_audit):
    # Exponential forgetting R(t) = exp(-t / S) with stability S in days
    reread = math.exp(-28 / 12.0) * 100
    capture_only = math.exp(-28 / (18.0 * avg_audit)) * 100 if avg_audit > 0 else 0.0
    if SKIP_7D_GATE:
        spaced = math.exp(-28 / (20.0 * max(avg_audit, 0.01))) * 100
    else:
        r7 = math.exp(-7 / (20.0 * max(avg_audit, 0.01)))
        s_after_7 = 95.0 * avg_audit * (0.55 + 0.45 * r7)
        spaced = math.exp(-21 / max(s_after_7, 0.01)) * 100
    return round(reread, 1), round(capture_only, 1), round(spaced, 1)

def main():
    print(f"Question Log Audit | Today: {TODAY} | Flags: delay={DELAYED_CAPTURE_HOURS}h generic={GENERIC_NOTES_MODE} skip7d={SKIP_7D_GATE}")
    print("-" * 78)
    scores, due_queue, domains = [], [], Counter()
    for row in RAW_LOG:
        logged, due = date.fromisoformat(row[0]), date.fromisoformat(row[6])
        if SKIP_7D_GATE:
            due = logged + timedelta(days=30)
        sc, flags = audit_row(row)
        scores.append(sc)
        domains[row[2][0]] += 1
        status = "PASS" if sc >= 0.9 else "WEAK"
        if due <= TODAY:
            due_queue.append((due, row[2], row[1], row[3], status))
        print(f"{row[2]} | L{row[3]} | audit={sc:.2f} (M={int(flags[0])} N={int(flags[1])} F={int(flags[2])}) | due={due} | {row[1][:38]}")
    avg_audit = sum(scores) / len(scores)
    reread, cap_only, spaced = simulate_4w_retention(avg_audit)
    print("-" * 78)
    print(f"Average engineer-voice audit score: {avg_audit:.2f} / 1.00")
    print(f"Due today ({TODAY}) or overdue: {len(due_queue)} items")
    for due, tid, q, lvl, st in sorted(due_queue):
        tag = "OVERDUE" if due < TODAY else "DUE-7D"
        print(f"  [{tag}] {tid} (L{lvl}, {st}): {q}")
    print(f"Top weak domains: {', '.join(f'{k}:{v}' for k, v in domains.most_common(3))}")
    print("4-Week (Day 28) Cold Recall Simulation:")
    print(f"  Re-read notes only:     {reread:5.1f}%")
    print(f"  Capture without retest: {cap_only:5.1f}%")
    print(f"  Log + spaced retest:    {spaced:5.1f}%")

if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()

Mixed review

Three questions from earlier topics. Nobody tells you the topic in an interview.

U01: Why does the curriculum schedule retests at 7 and 30 days instead of reviewing every topic again the very next day?
Next-day review tests short-term working memory where retrieval feels easy and barely increases stability. Spacing to 7 and 30 days forces effortful retrieval right as decay sets in, which produces durable long-term retention.
V01: What four elements separate an engineer-voiced answer from a business-voiced answer?
A concrete mechanism (how it works), a number (limit, cost, or latency), a failure mode (where it breaks), and a tradeoff (what you pay for the gain). Business framing may follow in one sentence, never replace them.
X01: How should you budget a 90-second three-level answer across Outcome, Mechanism, and Internals and limits?
About 20 seconds on Level 1 (Outcome: what it does and why it matters), 45 seconds on Level 2 (Mechanism: step-by-step how it works), and 25 seconds on Level 3 (Internals and limits: numbers, failure modes, and boundaries).

Short notes

Quick-memorization cheat sheet covering every subtopic on this page and the key facts to remember cold.

All subtopics on this page

  • 7-column schema: Log Date, Question, Topic, Level reached, What was missing, Model answer, Retest due so every row carries a cold cue, a grading target, and a retrieval gate.
  • Same-day capture (<24h): Record exact prompt wording and the exact missing mechanism within 24 hours; waiting blurs the gap into a useless topic label.
  • Engineer-voice model answer: Audit column 6 for mechanism + number + failure mode in 2 to 4 sentences before closing the session.
  • 7d and 30d retest scheduler: Speak cold against a 90-second clock; day-7 retrieval lifts 28-day recall from 17.4% to 74.7% in the kata, and steady-state load is M * (1/p + 1) items/week.
  • Weak-domain triage: Sort overdue rows first and aggregate misses by domain prefix (e.g. G:2, K:2, X:1) to pick your next mock focus.
Important things to remember
  • Memory hook (C-M-R-G): Catch (<24h exact gap), Model (Mech + Num + Fail), Retest (7d cold), Graduate (30d Solid or Shaky loop).
  • Workload formula: Weekly retest items = M * (1/p + 1); keep 7-day pass rate p >= 70% and daily new captures to 3 to 5 so weekly load stays under ~35 minutes.
  • Passing bar: Level 2 or higher spoken cold with numbers right; if you read the model answer first, the retest does not count.
  • Primary failure modes: Write-only logs (no 7d gate), generic labels ("Studied KV cache"), and business-voiced model answers (like K07 scoring 0.33).
  • Anki vs Question Log: Anki drills atomic facts (<25 words); Question Log drills 90-second spoken synthesis and real follow-up pressure.
