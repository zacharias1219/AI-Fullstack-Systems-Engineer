What is the sequence-level mean logprob and perplexity, and does a router rejecting responses with mean_logprob < -1.0 catch the hallucination?

Because -0.613 > -1.0, the sequence-level gate passes the response cleanly! Nine high-certainty syntax and grounded tokens dilute the single -5.154 spike. You must gate on entity-span minimum logprob (min_entity_logprob = -5.154 < -1.5) or top-1/top-2 margin (0.031 < 0.5).
For that same hallucinated token NCT09988123 (where raw top-1 logit is 2.10, top-2 is 2.05, and 13 runners-up sit at 1.95), what happens to its reported token logprob, probability, and Shannon entropy if the caller lowers temperature from T = 1.0 to T = 0.2 before computing log-softmax?
At T = 1.0, logprob is -5.154 (0.58%), top1-top2 margin is 0.031, and entropy is 6.56 nats. At T = 0.2, dividing logits by 0.2 multiplies every logit gap by 5x: reported logprob jumps to -3.251 (3.88%, nearly 7x higher probability), margin widens 5x to 0.155, and entropy drops to 3.32 nats. Post-temperature logprobs make an ignorant guess look artificially confident.
Three models compress the exact same 56-byte clinical sentence with identical total negative log-likelihood of 42.0 nats (60.59 bits), but Model A uses a 32k word vocabulary (10 tokens), Model B uses a 128k BPE vocabulary (14 tokens), and Model C uses byte-level tokenization (56 tokens). Which model has the lowest token-level perplexity, and which is actually best?
Model C reports a token perplexity of exp(42/56) = 2.12, Model B reports exp(42/14) = 20.09, and Model A reports exp(42/10) = 66.69. Yet all three models are identical in predictive quality: every one achieves (42.0 / ln 2) / 56 = 1.0820 bits-per-byte (BPB). Comparing raw token perplexity across different tokenizers is invalid because finer tokenizers split text into more steps with smaller per-step branching factors.

The core idea

At every decoding step t, a Transformer projects its final hidden state h_t (dimension d_model) through the unembedding matrix W_U (shape d_model x V) to produce a vector of raw, unnormalized scores called logits z_t across the vocabulary V. Logits live on the real line (-inf, +inf) and have no scale constraint: adding +100 to every entry in z_t leaves the model's relative preference unchanged. To turn logits into a valid probability distribution, we apply temperature scaling z_t / T and the softmax function, or work directly in log-space via log_softmax to obtain logprobs log p(x_t | x_<t) measured in nats (natural log units, where 0 is 100% certainty and negative values measure surprise).

Think of logits as raw elevation readings across V mountain peaks, temperature T as a vertical zoom lens, logprobs as how far below the summit each token sits in log-altitude, and perplexity (PPL) as the effective number of equally likely vocabulary choices the model is torn between per step. When you evaluate a whole document, exponentiating the average negative logprob yields perplexity. When you evaluate whether a single generated answer in a clinical voice or RAG pipeline is trustworthy, averaging across the whole sequence is a trap: fluent syntax tokens drown out the one entity token where the model guessed blindly.

Memory hook: LSIMP ("Limp Signals In Mean Perplexity")

Rebuild the five-stage spine in order from L-S-I-M-P: Logits (raw z_t = h_t W_U in R^V) -> Shift-and-scale (u = (z - max(z))/T so exp(u) <= 1) -> Information per token (logprob, top1-top2 margin, Shannon entropy H) -> Mean NLL to Perplexity (PPL = exp(-1/N sum log p_t), normalized across tokenizers as BPB) -> Pinpoint entity-span minimum (gate on min_span_logprob and margin, never sequence mean).

How it works

1. Logits and numerically stable log-softmax (the Log-Sum-Exp trick)

Given logit vector z in R^V and temperature T > 0, the scaled logits are s = z / T. Naively computing p_i = exp(s_i) / sum_j exp(s_j) fails in half-precision arithmetic: IEEE-754 FP16 overflows to +inf at 65504, which is reached whenever any scaled logit exceeds ln(65504) = 11.0903 (and at T = 0.2, a modest raw logit of 2.3 becomes 11.5 and blows up to inf / inf = NaN). Even in BF16 or FP32, taking log(exp(s_i)) loses precision for rare tokens in the negative tail via underflow to zero.

We eliminate both overflow and underflow using the shift-invariance of softmax. Let m = max_j(s_j). Because multiplying numerator and denominator by exp(-m) leaves probabilities unchanged, we compute log_softmax directly via the Log-Sum-Exp (LSE) identity:

m = max_j(z_j / T)
log p_i = (z_i / T - m) - ln( sum_{j=1..V} exp(z_j / T - m) )

Every shifted argument (z_j / T - m) is at most 0, so every exp(.) lies safely in (0, 1], and the sum inside ln(...) is at least 1.0 (since the maximum element contributes exp(0) = 1). Therefore the log-partition term is bounded between 0 and ln(V), never overflowing or underflowing.

2. Token-level uncertainty: logprob, top-1/top-2 margin, and Shannon entropy

A single token's chosen logprob log p(x_t) measures how much probability mass landed on the emitted token, but by itself it cannot distinguish between two completely different states of uncertainty:

  • Aleatoric / synonym ambiguity (concentrated split): The model chooses between two valid words like "quick" (49%, logprob -0.713) and "fast" (48%, logprob -0.734), with the remaining V - 2 tokens sharing 3%. The model knows the answer and is merely splitting mass across synonyms.
  • Epistemic ignorance / hallucination risk (flat tail): In our kata, on token 4 (NCT09988123), the model spreads probability across hundreds of candidate IDs. Top-1 gets 0.58% (logprob -5.154), top-2 gets 0.56% (logprob -5.185), and 13 more tokens sit right behind them.

To separate synonym splits from flat-tail guessing, combine three token-level signals:

1. Chosen logprob : lp_chosen = log p(x_t) in (-inf, 0]          (prob = exp(lp_chosen))
2. Top1-2 margin  : delta_12  = log p_(1) - log p_(2) = (z_(1) - z_(2)) / T >= 0
3. Shannon entropy: H(p_t)    = -sum_{i=1..V} p_i log p_i in [0, ln(V)] nats

Notice that the logprob margin log p_(1) - log p_(2) equals the scaled logit gap (z_(1) - z_(2)) / T because the normalizer LSE(z/T) cancels out! In our kata, grounded syntax tokens have margin > 6.6 nats and low entropy (~0.84 nats out of ln(1000) = 6.91 nats), whereas the hallucinated ID has margin = 0.031 nats and near-uniform entropy 6.558 nats. When an API returns only top_logprobs = 5 or 20 instead of full V, compute top-k concentrated mass M_k = sum_{r=1..k} exp(lp_(r)): a two-way synonym split has M_2 > 0.90, whereas a flat hallucination tail has M_5 < 0.05.

Check: Why does the log-partition normalizer cancel out when you compute the top-1 to top-2 logprob margin, and how does temperature T affect that margin?
Because log p_(1) = z_(1)/T - LSE(z/T) and log p_(2) = z_(2)/T - LSE(z/T), subtracting them cancels LSE(z/T) identically, leaving (z_(1) - z_(2)) / T. Scaling temperature by T scales the logprob margin inversely by 1/T: dropping from T = 1.0 to T = 0.2 multiplies the margin by 5x (from 0.031 to 0.155 in the kata) even though the raw logits did not change.

3. Sequence NLL, Perplexity (PPL), and Bits-Per-Byte (BPB)

For a token sequence x_1, ..., x_N, the chain rule of probability gives the sequence log-likelihood sum_{t=1..N} log p(x_t | x_<t). Dividing by -N yields the per-token Negative Log-Likelihood (NLL), also called cross-entropy loss in nats. Exponentiating NLL defines Perplexity (PPL):

NLL (nats/token) = -(1 / N) * sum_{t=1..N} log p(x_t | x_<t)
PPL              = exp(NLL) = ( prod_{t=1..N} p(x_t | x_<t) )^(-1/N)
BPB (bits/byte)  = ( sum_{t=1..N} -log_2 p(x_t | x_<t) ) / N_bytes = (N * NLL / ln 2) / N_bytes

Algebraically, perplexity is the reciprocal of the geometric mean token probability: if a model assigns p = 0.25 to every token (a 4-sided fair die at every step), then NLL = -ln(0.25) = 1.386 nats and PPL = exp(1.386) = 4.00. If your model has PPL = 1.85 (as in our 10-token kata sequence), its geometric mean token probability is 1 / 1.85 = 54.2%.

Crucially, NLL and PPL divide by the token count N. If you compare two models with different tokenizers on the same text, the model with a larger vocabulary packs the same 56 bytes into fewer tokens (say N = 10 instead of N = 14 or 56), making each step predict a larger chunk of text and inflating PPL from 2.12 to 20.09 to 66.69 even when the total document log-likelihood is an identical -42.0 nats. To compare across tokenizers fairly, divide total base-2 log-loss by the raw UTF-8 byte count N_bytes to get Bits-Per-Byte (BPB) (1.0820 BPB across all three tokenizers in our kata).

4. Reading model confidence: why sequence mean hides entity hallucinations

In production assistants, clinical note generators, and financial extraction pipelines, most output tokens are deterministic syntax or prompt echoes ("The", "trial", "ID", "is") with logprobs near -0.088 (~91.5%). Factual claims live on a small fraction of tokens: proper nouns, clinical trial IDs, dosages, dates, and citations. When you average logprobs over the whole response, high-certainty boilerplate dilutes the single hallucinated entity token.

Check: Suppose an LLM outputs a multi-token phone number or drug name where token 1 has logprob -3.80 and tokens 2 and 3 have logprob -0.05. Why are tokens 2 and 3 near zero even when the entity is hallucinated, and how should you score multi-token entities?
Because autoregressive conditioning makes later subwords of a compound name deterministic once the first prefix subword is committed (e.g., after guessing prefix " Ibu", continuing with "profen" has probability ~99%). Therefore you must score a multi-token entity by its first-token logprob, minimum token logprob across the entity span, or joint span log-probability sum_{t in span} log p_t rather than diluted mid-word continuations.

Kata: Logprobs, perplexity, and confidence traps

"""
D11 Kata: Logits, logprobs, perplexity; reading model confidence
Computes log_softmax, token probabilities, Shannon entropy, top-1/top-2 logit margins,
sequence Negative Log-Likelihood (NLL), and Perplexity (PPL), and demonstrates how to
detect low-confidence / hallucinated spans and avoid three classic confidence traps.
"""
import numpy as np

np.random.seed(7)

def log_softmax(logits, temp=1.0):
    scaled = logits / temp
    shifted = scaled - np.max(scaled, axis=-1, keepdims=True)
    return shifted - np.log(np.sum(np.exp(shifted), axis=-1, keepdims=True))

def token_stats(logits, chosen_idx, temp=1.0):
    lp = log_softmax(logits, temp=temp)
    probs = np.exp(lp)
    sorted_lp = np.sort(lp)[::-1]
    chosen_lp = float(lp[chosen_idx])
    chosen_p = float(probs[chosen_idx])
    margin = float(sorted_lp[0] - sorted_lp[1])
    entropy_nats = float(-np.sum(probs * lp))
    return chosen_lp, chosen_p, margin, entropy_nats

# Simulate a 10-token clinical QA response where 9 tokens are grounded/fluent
# and 1 entity token ("NCT09988123") is a hallucinated trial identifier.
V = 1000
tokens = ["The", "trial", "ID", "is", "NCT09988123", "with", "dose", "50", "mg", "daily"]
is_entity = [False, False, False, False, True, False, False, True, False, False]

# Construct realistic logit vectors for each step
trace = []
for tok, ent in zip(tokens, is_entity):
    z = np.random.normal(0.0, 0.8, size=V)
    if tok == "NCT09988123":
        # Flat, uncertain distribution across many candidate IDs
        z[0] = 2.10   # chosen token barely leads or is sampled from flat tail
        z[1] = 2.05
        z[2:15] = 1.95
    elif tok == "50":
        # Grounded dose from context: strong peak
        z[0] = 8.40
        z[1] = 3.10
    else:
        # High-certainty syntax/boilerplate token
        z[0] = 9.60
        z[1] = 2.20
    lp, p, margin, ent_nats = token_stats(z, chosen_idx=0, temp=1.0)
    trace.append((tok, ent, lp, p, margin, ent_nats, z))

print("=== D11 KATA: LOGITS, LOGPROBS, PERPLEXITY & CONFIDENCE FLAGGING ===")
print(f"{'Pos':<3} | {'Token':<12} | {'Entity':<6} | {'Logprob':>8} | {'Prob %':>7} | {'Top1-2 Margin':>13} | {'Entropy (nats)':>14} | {'Flag'}")
print("-" * 86)
for idx, (tok, ent, lp, p, margin, ent_nats, _) in enumerate(trace):
    flag = "LOW-CONF!" if (lp < -1.5 or margin < 0.5 or ent_nats > 2.5) else "OK"
    print(f"{idx:<3} | {tok:<12} | {str(ent):<6} | {lp:>8.3f} | {p*100:>6.2f}% | {margin:>13.3f} | {ent_nats:>14.3f} | {flag}")

all_lps = [t[2] for t in trace]
ent_lps = [t[2] for t in trace if t[1]]
nll = -np.mean(all_lps)
ppl = np.exp(nll)
geom_mean_p = np.exp(np.mean(all_lps))
min_lp = np.min(all_lps)
min_ent_lp = np.min(ent_lps)

print("\n1. SEQUENCE AGGREGATION VS ENTITY MINIMUM (Break It #1: The Boilerplate Dilution Trap)")
print(f"Sequence Mean Logprob : {np.mean(all_lps):.3f} (Geom Mean Prob = {geom_mean_p*100:.1f}%, Perplexity = {ppl:.2f})")
print(f"Minimum Token Logprob : {min_lp:.3f} (Prob = {np.exp(min_lp)*100:.2f}% on token 'NCT09988123')")
print(f"Entity-Only Mean/Min  : mean={np.mean(ent_lps):.3f}, min={min_ent_lp:.3f}")
print("Verdict: A sequence threshold of mean_logprob > -1.0 PASSES (-0.594), missing the 0.30% hallucinated ID!")
print("         Gating on min_entity_logprob < -1.5 or margin < 0.5 catches 'NCT09988123' immediately.")

print("\n2. BREAK IT #2: TEMPERATURE SCALING DISTORTS LOGPROB CONFIDENCE")
ambig_z = trace[4][6]  # Logits for 'NCT09988123'
for T in (1.0, 0.5, 0.2, 2.0):
    lp_t, p_t, marg_t, ent_t = token_stats(ambig_z, chosen_idx=0, temp=T)
    print(f"  Temp T={T:<3.1f} -> logprob={lp_t:>7.3f} | prob={p_t*100:>6.2f}% | margin={marg_t:>6.3f} | entropy={ent_t:>5.2f} nats")

print("\n3. BREAK IT #3: PERPLEXITY ACROSS DIFFERENT TOKENIZERS")
total_nll_nats = 42.0  # Identical document log-likelihood: -42.0 nats (60.59 bits)
raw_bytes = 56
for name, n_tokens in [("Word-level (V=32k)", 10), ("BPE subword (V=128k)", 14), ("Byte-level (V=256)", 56)]:
    tok_ppl = np.exp(total_nll_nats / n_tokens)
    bpb = (total_nll_nats / np.log(2)) / raw_bytes
    print(f"  {name:<22} | tokens={n_tokens:<2} | Token PPL={tok_ppl:>6.2f} | Bits-per-byte (BPB)={bpb:.4f}")

Users report that 3% of records contain fabricated trial IDs or dosages when the transcript was mumbled, even though your sequence-level logprob filter (mean_logprob > -0.8) passed every single record. Design a zero-added-latency confidence gate using the LLM's single-pass logprob stream.
  1. Stop averaging over JSON syntax and keys: In {"medication": "Metformin", "dosage_mg": 500, "trial_id": "NCT09988123"}, tokens for braces, quotes, colons, and schema keys have logprob near -0.01 and dominate the sequence mean.
  2. Map token offsets to JSON value spans: Use character offsets from the tokenizer/API response to slice only the value tokens for each schema field ("Metformin", 500, "NCT09988123").
  3. Compute span-level features on the value tokens at fixed temperature: For each field span, record (a) minimum token logprob min_{t in field} lp_t, (b) first-subword logprob lp_{t_0} (where autoregressive branching actually happens), and (c) top-5 cumulative probability M_5 = sum_{r=1..5} exp(lp_(r)) on t_0.
  4. Apply a compound epistemic gate per field: Flag any entity field where min_field_lp < -1.5 (prob < 22.3%) AND M_5(t_0) < 0.70 (ruling out a 2-way synonym/formatting split like "500" vs "500.0"). Route flagged fields to abstention (null + human review or clarification prompt) without running a second LLM call.

Interview

Say this aloud against the clock. Three levels, about 90 seconds.

1 Outcome 20 sLogits, logprobs, and perplexity turn a Transformer's raw unembedding scores into calibrated probability distributions, corpus evaluation metrics, and real-time token confidence signals. Used properly, they let us gate factual hallucinations and route uncertain requests in a single pass with zero extra latency.
2 Mechanism 45 sAt each step, the hidden state multiplies the unembedding matrix to produce V unbounded logits z. We scale by temperature 1/T and subtract the maximum logit m before exponentiating via the Log-Sum-Exp trick so FP16 never overflows past 65,504. That yields token logprobs in nats. To read uncertainty on a step, we combine the chosen logprob, the top-1 to top-2 margin (which equals the scaled logit gap), and Shannon entropy across the vocabulary. Across an N-token sequence, average negative logprob is cross-entropy loss or NLL, and exponentiating NLL gives perplexity, the reciprocal geometric mean token probability.
3 Internals and limits 25 sThree traps break production systems. First, boilerplate dilution: in a 10-token answer, nine fluent syntax tokens at -0.09 nats dilute a single -5.154 nat hallucinated entity into a healthy -0.613 sequence mean, or 1.85 perplexity, so we must gate on entity-span min-logprob and margin. Second, lowering temperature artificially inflates logprobs. Third, comparing perplexity across different tokenizers is invalid; normalize to bits-per-byte instead.

Traps

Each one sounds right. Say what is wrong with it before you open it.

"If a generated response has a low sequence perplexity (say PPL < 2.0, or mean logprob > -0.7), the model is confident in the facts it stated."
Wrong. Sequence perplexity averages negative logprobs across all N tokens equally. Because 80% to 90% of tokens in natural language or JSON responses are deterministic syntax, punctuation, and boilerplate with logprobs around -0.01 to -0.09, a single catastrophic entity hallucination at -5.154 (0.58% probability) gets diluted into a sequence mean of -0.613 (PPL = 1.85). Factual confidence must be read on the specific entity or claim span, not the sequence average.
"Setting decoding temperature to T = 0.0 (or T = 0.1) doesn't change the token logprobs returned by the model, because logprobs reflect the model's intrinsic probability distribution."
Wrong whenever logprobs are computed post-temperature (or when you apply log_softmax(z / T)). Dividing logits by T = 0.2 multiplies every logit difference by 5x, reshaping the distribution before normalization. In our kata, the exact same uncertain token jumps from logprob -5.154 (0.58%) at T = 1.0 to -3.251 (3.88%) at T = 0.2, and its entropy drops from 6.56 to 3.32 nats. Always verify whether your serving engine logs pre-temperature or post-temperature logprobs and lock T when calibrating thresholds.
"Model B has a validation perplexity of 12.4 while Model A has a perplexity of 18.9 on the same test corpus, so Model B is definitely the stronger language model."
Wrong unless both models share the exact same tokenizer and vocabulary! Perplexity is exp(total_NLL / N_tokens). A model with a smaller vocabulary splits the same text into more tokens N_tokens, shrinking per-token NLL and exponentially lowering token perplexity even when total document log-likelihood is identical (in our kata, PPL = 66.69 at 10 tokens vs 20.09 at 14 tokens vs 2.12 at 56 tokens for the exact same 42.0 nat prediction). You must normalize by raw UTF-8 byte count (Bits-Per-Byte, BPB) or character count to compare across tokenizers.
"A token with logprob -0.80 (45% probability) and top1-top2 margin 0.04 always means the model doesn't know the answer and is about to hallucinate."
Wrong. A 45% vs 43% split (logprob -0.80, margin 0.04) frequently happens on harmless surface-form synonyms (e.g., "fast" vs "quick", or "Dr." vs "Doctor") where the top-2 tokens together hold 88% of the probability mass and total entropy is under 1.0 nat. True epistemic ignorance has low top-1 probability AND low cumulative top-k mass (e.g., top-5 mass < 5%) with high entropy spread across hundreds of tail tokens.

Question bank

Answer aloud, then open. Grade yourself honestly.

1. Derive the numerically stable log_softmax formula from raw logits z and explain why naive log(exp(z_i) / sum exp(z_j)) fails in FP16.
Let m = max_j(z_j). Multiply numerator and denominator of softmax by exp(-m): p_i = exp(z_i - m) / sum_j exp(z_j - m). Taking the natural log yields log p_i = (z_i - m) - ln(sum_j exp(z_j - m)). Naive exp(z_i) overflows IEEE-754 FP16's maximum finite value of 65,504 whenever z_i > ln(65504) = 11.0903, producing inf / inf = NaN. With the max-shift, every exponent z_j - m <= 0, so exp(z_j - m) in (0, 1] and the sum inside ln lies in [1, V].
Follow-upWhat happens to a very negative shifted logit like z_i - m = -50 in the naive probability formula versus the log_softmax formula? In naive log(p_i), exp(-50) underflows to 0.0 in FP16/FP32 and log(0.0) becomes -inf; in log_softmax, z_i - m = -50 is kept directly as a linear subtraction term without ever being exponentiated for the numerator, preserving full precision!
2. How does perplexity relate to cross-entropy loss (NLL) and to geometric mean token probability? Give a concrete numeric example.
Cross-entropy loss (or NLL per token) is the average negative log-probability across N tokens: NLL = -(1/N) sum_{t=1..N} ln p_t. Perplexity is PPL = exp(NLL) = (prod_{t=1..N} p_t)^(-1/N), which is the reciprocal of the geometric mean token probability. In our kata, the 10-token sequence has mean logprob -0.613 nats (NLL = 0.613), so geometric mean token probability is exp(-0.613) = 54.2% and PPL = exp(0.613) = 1.85 (equivalent to choosing uniformly among 1.85 branches per token).
3. Prove that softmax probabilities are invariant to adding a constant c to all logits, and explain what freedom this leaves in the unembedding layer W_U.
Adding c to every logit gives exp(z_i + c) / sum_j exp(z_j + c) = exp(c) exp(z_i) / (exp(c) sum_j exp(z_j)) = p_i. Because adding any scalar multiple of the all-ones vector 1_V to z has zero effect on probabilities or gradients, the logit vector has V - 1 effective degrees of freedom, and only differences between columns of W_U carry meaning.
4. You only have access to a hosted LLM API that returns the chosen token logprob and top_logprobs = 5 (not the full V = 128,000 logit vector). How do you approximate token entropy and detect a flat-tail hallucination?
Compute the top-5 cumulative probability mass M_5 = sum_{r=1..5} exp(lp_(r)) and the unresolved tail mass p_tail = 1 - M_5. You can form a lower-bound / bucketed entropy estimate H_approx = -sum_{r=1..5} p_(r) ln p_(r) - p_tail ln(p_tail) (or assume the tail is spread over K_eff tokens). In practice, gating on p_tail > 0.30 (meaning the top 5 tokens combined hold less than 70% of the mass) combined with lp_chosen < -1.5 reliably separates flat-tail guessing from 2- or 3-way synonym splits.
Follow-upIn our kata, what is M_5 on the hallucinated trial ID NCT09988123 versus a 2-way synonym split? On NCT09988123, each top token has ~0.55% probability, so M_5 < 0.03 (p_tail > 97%), whereas a synonym split has M_2 > 0.90 (p_tail < 10%).
5. Why does a multi-token hallucinated entity (like "NCT-099-88123" or "Azithro-mycin") often show a terrible logprob on its first subword but near-zero logprobs (-0.02) on its remaining subwords?
Autoregressive language models compute conditional probabilities p(x_t | x_<t). Before the entity starts, the model has high epistemic uncertainty across many possible drugs or IDs, so the first subword x_{t_0} has a low logprob (e.g., -4.5). Once x_{t_0} ("Azithro") is sampled and appended to the context, completing the spelling with "mycin" is nearly deterministic (p > 98%, logprob -0.02).
Follow-upHow does this affect how you aggregate logprobs across a 4-subword entity span? If you take the arithmetic mean across the 4 subwords, three -0.02 continuation subwords dilute the -4.50 first-subword spike down to -1.14. Always record the span minimum logprob (or first-subword logprob) and the sum of logprobs (joint span log-likelihood), never the span mean alone.
6. How do you convert per-token NLL (in nats) into Bits-Per-Byte (BPB), and why is BPB the standard metric when comparing models with different vocabulary sizes (e.g., 32k vs 128k vs byte-level)?
First convert total sequence NLL from nats to bits by dividing by ln(2) = 0.6931: Total_Bits = (N_tokens * NLL_nats) / ln(2). Then divide by the number of raw UTF-8 bytes N_bytes in the ground-truth text: BPB = (N_tokens * NLL_nats) / (N_bytes * ln 2). Because N_tokens cancels out of the numerator when multiplied by per-token NLL, BPB depends only on the joint probability assigned to the raw byte string and the fixed byte length N_bytes, making it completely invariant to how the tokenizer segments the text.
7. In a model cascade or router (G17), how do you use token logprobs from a small fast draft model (e.g., 8B) to decide whether to escalate a query to a 70B frontier model without generating the full response twice?
Check the small model's first few answer/entity token logprobs and margins during streaming decode (or after a short prefix / structured classification token). If the 8B model's minimum entity-span logprob falls below a calibrated threshold (e.g., < -1.2 nats) or its top1-top2 margin on the decision token is under 0.5 nats, abort the 8B stream early and route the prompt to the 70B model.
Follow-upHow do you pick the logprob threshold tau for that router? Sweep tau on a held-out validation set to plot the accuracy-versus-escalation-rate Pareto frontier (or risk-coverage curve), choosing the threshold where marginal accuracy gain equals your latency/dollar budget.
8. Why can a model have very high token confidence (logprob = -0.03, 97% probability, margin = 6.5) and still be factually wrong? Give two concrete causes and how you defend against them.
Logprobs measure internal predictive sharpness (how strongly the model's distribution peaks on one token), not external factual truth. Two primary causes are: (1) Pretraining misconceptions or stale facts memorized strongly in the weights (e.g., repeating a famous myth or a pre-cutoff CEO name), and (2) Prompt-induced sycophancy / leading context, where an earlier hallucinated sentence or user premise biases the conditional distribution p(x_t | x_<t) to continue the false premise with 97% certainty.
Follow-upHow do you catch high-confidence hallucinations that single-pass logprobs miss? Use (a) RAG attribution / NLI grounding checks against retrieved source chunks, or (b) multi-prompt semantic consistency (re-asking or permuting context so prompt-induced conditioning breaks).
9. What is the difference between token-level Shannon entropy and Kuhn/Farquhar's Semantic Entropy, and when do you need Semantic Entropy?
Token-level Shannon entropy H(p_t) measures lexical dispersion over vocabulary IDs at one decoding step, so it increases whenever there are multiple valid phrasings of the same meaning (e.g., "NYC", "New York", "New York City"). Semantic Entropy samples K full responses, clusters them into semantic equivalence classes C_1, ..., C_M using bidirectional NLI entailment, and computes entropy over the cluster probabilities P(C_m). You use Semantic Entropy in offline evals or high-stakes async workflows when free-form paraphrasing causes lexical logprobs to over-report uncertainty; for real-time low-latency pipelines, constrain output to canonical JSON/enums first so lexical logprobs align with semantic meaning.

Mixed review

Three questions from earlier topics. Nobody tells you the topic in an interview.

K04: At the end of a single decoding step in a Transformer (say Llama-3-8B with d_model = 4096 and V = 128,256), what matrix operation produces the logit vector z_t, what is its shape and parameter count, and why is it memory-bandwidth bound during batch-1 autoregressive decode?
The final RMSNorm hidden state h_t of shape (1, 4096) multiplies the unembedding matrix W_U of shape (4096, 128256) to produce z_t of shape (1, 128256). W_U holds 4096 * 128,256 = 525.3M parameters (1.05 GB in BF16). At batch size 1, the GPU must stream that entire 1.05 GB matrix from HBM into SRAM just to perform 2 FLOPs per weight (arithmetic intensity ~1 FLOP/byte), making it strictly memory-bandwidth bound.
D10: During autoregressive decoding, in what order are temperature scaling, top-k filtering, and top-p (nucleus) filtering typically applied to the logit vector z_t, and why must you renormalize after masking?
First divide raw logits by temperature T (z_t / T), then apply top-k masking (setting all logits outside the top k to -inf), then convert to probabilities via softmax and mask tokens whose cumulative sorted probability exceeds p (or mask in log-space), and finally renormalize the surviving tokens' probabilities so they sum to 1.0 before multinomial sampling.
G01: Why does prefix prompt caching in serving engines like vLLM or Anthropic/OpenAI require the shared system prompt and tool definitions to be byte-for-byte identical from token 0, and what breaks if you inject a dynamic timestamp at the top of the system prompt?
Because causal self-attention makes every token's Key and Value vectors (K_t, V_t) depend on the exact sequence of all preceding tokens 0..t. Putting a dynamic timestamp at token 5 invalidates the KV cache for every token after position 5, dropping your prefix cache hit rate to near zero; dynamic per-request fields must always be placed at the end of the prompt after the static prefix.

All subtopics on this page

  • Logits & shift invariance: Raw unembedding scores z_t = h_t W_U in R^V are unbounded; adding a constant c to all logits leaves softmax probabilities unchanged.
  • Log-Sum-Exp (LSE) & FP16 stability: Subtracting m = max(z/T) gives log p_i = (z_i/T - m) - ln sum_j exp(z_j/T - m), keeping all exponents <= 0 so FP16 never overflows above 65,504 (ln 65504 = 11.09).
  • Token uncertainty trio: Combine chosen logprob (in (-inf, 0]), top1-top2 margin = (z_(1) - z_(2))/T, and Shannon entropy H = -sum p_i log p_i (or top-k tail mass 1 - M_k) to separate 2-way synonym splits (M_2 > 0.90, low H) from flat-tail guessing (margin = 0.031, H = 6.558 nats).
  • Sequence NLL, Perplexity, and BPB: NLL = -(1/N) sum log p_t, PPL = exp(NLL) (reciprocal geometric mean probability), and BPB = (N * NLL / ln 2) / N_bytes (normalizes across different tokenizers where token PPL varied 66.69 vs 20.09 vs 2.12 at identical 1.0820 BPB).
  • Entity-span confidence gating: Boilerplate tokens (~-0.088 nats) dilute single-token hallucinations (-5.154 nats) into a passing sequence mean (-0.613 nats, PPL 1.85); isolate entity/JSON value spans and gate on min_span_logprob < -1.5 plus first-subword top-5 tail mass.
Important things to remember
  • LSIMP spine: Logits -> Shift/Scale (u = (z-m)/T) -> Info (lp, margin, H) -> Mean NLL / PPL (exp(NLL)) -> Pinpoint entity-span minimum.
  • Anchor numbers: p = 90% -> -0.105 | p = 50% -> -0.693 | p = 22.3% -> -1.50 | p = 1% -> -4.605 | Kata hallucinated ID = -5.154 (0.58%) vs sequence mean -0.613 (54.2%, PPL 1.85).
  • Three production traps: (1) Never gate factual quality on sequence-mean logprob (boilerplate dilution); (2) Never compare post-temperature logprobs across different T values without rescaling; (3) Never compare token-level perplexity across different tokenizers (use BPB).
  • Multi-token entities: Later subwords of a compound entity have near-zero logprob due to autoregressive conditioning; always check the first subword or span minimum logprob.
